\section*{AI Use Statement}
Large language models were used in two distinct roles in this work.
First, frontier LLMs (\texttt{claude-cli} \texttt{haiku}/\texttt{sonnet} and \texttt{codex-cli}) served directly as the experimental subjects (autonomous research executors, independent auditors, and condition-blind judges) evaluated across all 112 controlled benchmark runs.
Second, AI coding and writing assistants aided in implementing boilerplate harness utilities, plotting scripts, and LaTeX prose refinement.
All experimental designs, statistical scripts, deterministic verification rules, unit tests, numbers, citations, and final manuscript claims were independently verified by the authors, including automated provenance auditing of this manuscript against \texttt{paper/out/stats\_summary.txt} and full-text verification of all cited references.

\section*{Impact Statement}
This work addresses the reliability and scientific integrity of autonomous LLM research agents by introducing open-source deterministic verification gates and path-isolated cross-model auditing.
Its primary societal benefit is reducing the risk that fabricated statistics, hardcoded baselines, or silent coding bugs enter scientific preprints and peer-review pipelines.
Conversely, automated governance should never be treated as a complete substitute for human scientific scrutiny: our own reverse cross-family experiments demonstrate that lenient LLM reviewers can overlook genuine defects, and deterministic checks remain bounded by their specified invariants.
We therefore release our tooling as a transparent, fail-closed aid for researchers and reviewers rather than an autonomous arbiter of scientific truth.
