\section*{AI Use Statement}
Large language models were used in two distinct roles in this work.
First, frontier LLMs (\texttt{claude-haiku}, \texttt{claude-sonnet}, and \texttt{codex-cli}) served directly as the experimental subjects (autonomous research executors and independent read-only auditors) evaluated across all 245 paired frozen-artifact trials and 21 unseen \textsc{SciIntegrity-Bench} blind-test runs.
Second, AI coding and writing assistants aided in implementing harness utilities, plotting scripts, and LaTeX prose refinement.
All experimental protocols, unified ground-truth rules, deterministic verification rules, unit tests, numbers, citations, and final manuscript claims were independently audited by the authors, including two internal hostile milestone audits that eliminated tautological leakage metrics, circular \texttt{H06} labeling, and trial-level pseudo-replication, plus automated provenance and writing verification via \texttt{./veriloop audit-paper}.

\section*{Impact Statement}
This work addresses the reliability and scientific integrity of autonomous LLM research agents by introducing open-source deterministic verification gates, dual-model milestone auditing, and a frozen SHA-256 evaluation benchmark.
Its primary positive impact is reducing the risk that arithmetic contradictions, synthetic data fallbacks, or subtle coding bugs enter scientific preprints and peer review, while discouraging circular or confounded benchmark evaluation practices.
Conversely, automated verification must never be mistaken for an infallible oracle of scientific truth: our unseen blind test documents residual non-log provenance false positives (0.69\% number-level FPR across 18 verified-clean runs, with out-of-sample recall unmeasured), and even strong LLM reviewers (\texttt{claude-sonnet}) missed natural denominator bugs.
We therefore release VeriLoop as a transparent, fail-closed aid for researchers and reviewers rather than an autonomous arbiter of scientific validity.
