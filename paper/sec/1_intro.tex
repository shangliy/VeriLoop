\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous scientific research agents now inspect datasets, implement models, compute statistics, and author complete manuscripts without human intervention~\citep{yang2026aris,luo2025pitfalls}, yet independent audits expose a persistent divide between fluent scientific prose and actual execution evidence.
In an audit of AI-generated papers, \textsc{FabScore}~\citep{chen2026fabscore} reports a 21.2\% claim-level fabrication rate, with AI reviewers overlooking 86.2\% of fabricated claims.
Similarly, \textsc{SciIntegrity-Bench}~\citep{yang2026sciintegrity} records a 34.2\% academic integrity failure rate across seven frontier LLMs.
In our own audited agent runs, one script divided segment residuals by the global sample size in \texttt{analyze\_flame.py}, silently corrupting every reported RMSE and AIC while exiting cleanly with code zero, while another report misreported a monthly sales total as \texttt{720.591} instead of the \texttt{720.587} sum of its logged monthly values.

Two recent governance paradigms attempt to close this gap, yet both leave critical failure modes unguarded (Figure~\ref{fig:arch}).
\textsc{EviBound}~\citep{chen2026evibound} enforces pre-execution acceptance schemas and post-execution artifact existence checks, reducing missing-file hallucinations to zero on eight self-authored tasks.
Because existence checks verify only process completion and file presence, they approve scripts that miscompute formulas, inject synthetic fallbacks, or contradict their own logs.
\textsc{ARIS}~\citep{yang2026aris} pairs a coding agent with a second model family across iterative review rounds to catch deeper flaws.
Yet \textsc{ARIS} leaves Stage-1 experiment auditing advisory, evaluates a single observational trajectory without controlled baselines, and does not pair deterministic provenance checks against independent ground-truth labels.

Evaluating research governance itself carries subtle methodological hazards because comparing a blocking switch against a non-blocking switch creates tautological leakage metrics, while re-running executors per reviewer condition confounds artifact drift with reviewer capability.
Circular ground-truth labeling poses a similar risk when an audit treats a checker's own strict logging rule as scientific misconduct on one split while calling equivalent analytic derivations benign false positives on another.
To address both the engineering gap and the evaluation gap, we present \textbf{VeriLoop}, an efficient dual-layer verification harness with a stdlib-only deterministic core paired with a publicly frozen SHA-256 evaluation benchmark.
Its deterministic first layer enforces cryptographic claim hashing, unit-normalized signed numerical provenance, and static Python AST checks at zero LLM cost before external review runs.
Because syntactic checks cannot detect flawed statistical formulas or unsupported causal leaps, our second layer invokes a read-only auditor on raw workspace artifacts and pairs with a dual-model milestone gate that audits intermediate outputs before downstream work proceeds.

Unlike \textsc{EviBound}, which checks only artifact existence, VeriLoop binds reported decimals and scripts to raw execution logs and AST hygiene rules while retaining semantic code auditing.
In contrast to \textsc{ARIS}, which reports an unverified observational run, we evaluate single layers and dual-layer combinations on identical SHA-256 frozen workspaces against explicit file-and-line ground truth, reporting precision, recall, F1, false-positive rate (FPR), clean-output yield, and dual-label sensitivity with zero post-hoc overrides.

We evaluate VeriLoop across repeated paired LLM audit trials on sixteen frozen workspaces authored by two executor families (nine clean and seven defective under a unified post-audit rule classifying disclosed, arithmetically correct derivations from logged counts as clean provenance gaps).
Deterministic Layer~1 blocks an in-sample 71.4\% of defective workspaces at zero LLM cost (four of seven directly attributable plus one incidental unit-rescaling block) while read-only \texttt{codex-cli} auditing catches the remaining logic and inferential defects for 100.0\% recall (Table~\ref{tab:primary}).
Hard-blocking on Layer-1 provenance warnings lowers clean yield to 77.8\% because strict log-only matching flags two benign non-log derivations or phrasings, quantifying a concrete precision-cost trade-off between strict hard blocking and cascading Layer-1 diagnostics into Layer~2.
True frozen-artifact pairing also isolates model-calibration differences on natural bugs (Table~\ref{tab:decoupling}), while prepending an executor summary shows no detectable degradation under a calibrated binary rubric (workspace-level McNemar $p=1.000000$).
Finally, locking our v2 mechanical gates by SHA-256 and testing them blind on twenty-one strictly unseen \textsc{SciIntegrity-Bench} scenarios yields zero AST false positives and a 0.69\% number-level FPR across eighteen verified-clean completed runs, with out-of-sample recall unmeasured because zero unseen runs triggered a benchmark trap.

Our contributions span four publicly reproducible deliverables.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1.5pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Dual-layer governance stack (\S\ref{sec:method}).} A stdlib-only deterministic core enforcing claim ledger gates, unit-normalized signed number tracing, and Python AST checks, paired with read-only auditing and dual-model milestone gates.
  \item \textbf{Frozen paired benchmark (\S\ref{sec:primary} and \S\ref{sec:decoupling}).} Sixteen SHA-256 frozen workspaces with unified ground-truth rules quantifying Layer-1 pre-filtering, strict-blocking yield trade-offs, and paired model-vs-framing behavior.
  \item \textbf{Unseen held-out mechanical calibration (\S\ref{sec:unseen}).} A SHA-256 locked blind test on twenty-one strictly unseen \textsc{SciIntegrity-Bench} scenarios measuring honest out-of-sample mechanical FPR with committed forensic artifacts.
  \item \textbf{Zero-override reproducible release (\S\ref{sec:dogfood}).} Committed frozen workspaces, locked v2 gate scripts, ground-truth CSV, raw trial JSONLs, offline tests, and a manuscript self-audited by its own provenance and writing linters.
\end{itemize}
